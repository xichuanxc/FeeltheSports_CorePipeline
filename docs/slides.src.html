Feeling the Tennis Game

Acoustic racket-strike detection for synchronised haptic feedback

Chuan Xi Supervised by: Assoc. Prof. David Nichols & Dr. Jemma König University of Waikato COMPX520 · 2026
The question

On a screen you can only see and hear

  • In person, you feel the physical energy of the game.
  • Hard hits and fast rallies most of all.
  • Through a screen, that channel is gone.
  • Giving it back is what Feel the Sports is for.
Research question Can we detect every racket strike in tennis audio, and drive a phone to vibrate in time with it?

And does that raise a viewer’s sense of immersion?

Traditional · where

Spectral-flux peak detection

  • Band-pass, then pick the sudden rises.
  • Finds 98% of events.
  • Says nothing about what any of them is.
Learned · what

Log-mel slice into a small CNN

  • 150 ms around each candidate, five classes.
  • 0.84 accuracy on a match never trained on.
  • Trained on exactly what peak detection surfaced.
Why both Peak detection is what makes a dataset exist at all. The model then hands the labelling back a suggestion that is right five times in six, so a person confirms instead of deciding.
What was built

The VideoPlayer program

Plays the match, serves the phones
  • Reads a timeline of events
  • Synchronised ticks with phones
  • A TCP Server for broadcasting events

Every phone gets the same timeline and is held to the same clock.

What was built

The FeeltheSports app

The client, in the hand
  • Finds the laptop by itself — nothing to pair, no address to type in
  • Buzzes on every racket strike, in time with the match
  • No add-on hardware — the phone’s own vibration motor
Samsung Galaxy A03

A budget phone, on purpose. If the effect only lands on a flagship, it does not reach the viewers this is for.

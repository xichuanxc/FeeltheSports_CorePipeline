Feeling the Tennis Game

Acoustic racket-strike detection for synchronised haptic feedback

Chuan Xi Supervised by: Assoc. Prof. David Nichols & Dr. Jemma König University of Waikato COMPX520 · 2026
The question

On a screen you can only see and hear

  • In person, you feel the physical energy of the game.
  • Hard hits and fast rallies most of all.
  • Through a screen, that channel is gone.
  • Giving it back is what Feel the Sports is for.
Research question Can we detect every racket strike in tennis audio, and drive a phone to vibrate in time with it?

And does that raise a viewer’s sense of immersion?
The question

Restoring it usually costs hardware

  • Vibration rigs, thermal modules, body suits.
  • The earlier Feel the Sports work: a wrist device.
  • You only get the feeling if you already bought something.
The device almost everyone already has 70% of people worldwide own a smartphone. Wearables are nowhere near that.
And deliberately a cheap one

Tested on a Samsung Galaxy A03, not a flagship. “Already own” has to mean already own.

Finding the hits

You just told them apart.
The detector cannot.

  • It looks for sudden rises in energy.
  • A squeak, a bounce, a seat banging: all sudden rises.
  • Finding a sound is not knowing what it is.
97% Detector recall of confirmed hits are found
46% Are actually hits 583 of 1,270 checked events — the rest are unwanted
Consequence Buzzing on everything it finds is not an option. What each sound is has to come from the sound itself.

Finding the hits

What the model adds

0.84 Accuracy · five classes on a match never trained on · sd 0.04 over 28 runs
0.88 F1 · racket hit the class the buzzes are made of · sd 0.06
The two stages do different jobs
  • Peak detection finds 98% of events, and says nothing about what they are.
  • The model says what — trained on exactly the events peak detection surfaced.
  • So labelling stops being a decision and becomes a confirmation, five times in six.
Three ways this flattered itself
  • Stopping training on the test match: +0.10.
  • Measuring on matches it trained on: +0.2 recall.
  • Reporting one run: folds move 0.10 between runs.

Used to filter playback instead, precision goes 44% to 96% for 20 points of recall — a different question.

Why per-broadcast
  • Videos differ hugely in loudness.
  • A fixed setting leaves one match dead, the next buzzing constantly.
The honest cost
  • So strength is relative, not absolute.
  • A soft hit in a quiet match can feel like a hard hit in a loud one.
Making it felt

The phone, and keeping it in time

Why not just say “buzz now”?
  • The phone is never told “buzz now”.
  • A message per hit would pick up every wobble in the Wi-Fi.
  • Sending it up front leaves the network only slow drift.
On the phone

An Android app, written for this. It holds the timeline and fires the vibrator itself.

Measured

Timing holds to single-digit milliseconds, laptop to phone.

The answer

How the study ran

  • Two clips: one with vibration, one without.
  • Order balanced across participants.
  • Sound on in both, as anyone would actually watch.
  • A questionnaire before, and a longer one after.
  • Ethics approved; participants identified only by code.

What twelve people said

4.67 of 5 · noticed, fits, in time nobody doubted the buzzes land in the right place
3.36 of 5 · enjoyed, engaging, would install six would install it, five would not
The answer that explains the split

“When I’m not focused it helps me focus. When I’m focused it distracts me from watching.”

Still open
  • Buzz strength is relative to each video.
  • Highlights, not live — and not yet running live either.
  • A bigger study — this seminar is a chance to recruit.

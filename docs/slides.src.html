Feeling the game

Acoustic racket-strike detection for synchronised haptic feedback

Chuan Xi Supervisors: David Nichols & Jemma König University of Waikato COMPX520 · 2026
The problem

Tennis is carried by its sound

The crack of the strings tells you the timing, the power, and whether the ball was struck cleanly — often before the picture does. Strip that channel away and a rally flattens into movement.

For deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers, it is simply absent.

Research question Can the impact moments of a match be recovered from broadcast audio accurately enough to drive vibration on a phone, in sync, in real time?
The bar to clear

A vibration that fires when nothing happened breaks the illusion far harder than one that never fires at all. Precision over recall — this constraint decides every choice that follows.

The sound

Your ear separates them instantly.
The detector cannot.

A band-passed spectral-flux detector with adaptive peak picking finds transients — sharp rises in energy. It is very good at that: it proposes 97% of every strike a human later confirmed.

But a shoe squeak, a bounce, a seat banging and a forehand are all transients. Detection is not classification.

97% Detector recall of confirmed strikes are proposed as candidates
46% Are actually hits 551 of 1,194 adjudicated events — the rest are nuisance
Consequence Fewer than half the transients survive human review as racket hits. Buzzing on all of them is not an option — so the discrimination has to come from the impact sound itself.
The sound

The same four, in log-mel

Broadband attack, fast decay, energy across the whole band.

Low-frequency thud, no frame resonance above it.

Horizontal friction bands that sustain, not a transient at all.

Diffuse and unstructured — energy everywhere, edges nowhere.

The model

Does it work?

0.87 F1 · racket hit on a match the model never saw in training or tuning
0.0 False buzzes / min median across matches, detector-gated at P ≥ 0.70
0.68 Strike recall roughly one hit in three still produces no vibration
The architectural finding Running the CNN over every 10 ms window costs 2.4 false buzzes per minute. Keeping the onset detector as a gate and asking the model only about candidates drops that to zero, at the same recall — and 180× less compute.
On honest evaluation Selecting the training epoch on the held-out match inflated macro-F1 by +0.10. A three-way split removed it. The corrected, lower numbers are the ones reported here.

Why per-broadcast

Broadcast mixes differ by tens of decibels. A fixed absolute mapping would render one match numb and another saturated.

The honest cost

Intensity is therefore relative. A soft hit in a quiet match can feel like a hard hit in a loud one. Cross-match calibration is unsolved.

Keeping a phone in sync with a laptop

The design decision The phone is never told “vibrate now”. It receives the whole timeline in advance, synchronises its clock, and schedules every pulse locally.

Pushing one message per event would inherit every millisecond of Wi-Fi jitter — and impact timing is exactly what the system exists to convey. Scheduling locally makes the network responsible only for drift, not for timing.

What 11 people said

The detector is not the problem Noticeable, fits, synchronised: 4.73 / 5, spread 0.52. Nobody disputed that the pulses land in the right place.
Wanting it is a separate question Enjoyed, engaging, would install: 3.39 / 5, spread 1.37. Six would install it, four would not.
Where it stands

What is not solved

Limitations
  • Roughly one strike in three produces no vibration.
  • Ball bounces on clay are physically undetectable by onset strength — prominence 0.169 against 0.168 for ambient noise.
  • Intensity is relative to each broadcast, not absolute.
  • For some viewers the vibration captured attention rather than supporting it — one participant stopped watching the match and waited for the next buzz.
  • The corpus is highlight packages, which carry far less commentary than a live broadcast.
Next
  • Targeted annotation of the under-represented classes to lift recall.
  • Export to ONNX and move inference on-device.
  • Intensity that separates a gentle rally from a smash — the most repeated request in the free-text answers.
  • A larger study: n = 11 shows the split, but cannot explain who falls on which side.
The principle that held throughout A false buzz is worse than a missed one.

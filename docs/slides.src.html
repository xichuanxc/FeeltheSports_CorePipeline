Feeling the Tennis Game

Acoustic racket-strike detection for synchronised haptic feedback

Chuan Xi Supervised by: Assoc. Prof. David Nichols & Dr. Jemma König University of Waikato COMPX520 · 2026
The problem

Tennis is carried by its sound

  • The strings carry the timing, the power, and whether the ball was hit cleanly.
  • You usually hear it before you see it.
  • Take it away and a rally becomes just movement.
  • For deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers, it is simply gone.
Research question Can we find the moment of every hit in TV audio, well enough to make a phone vibrate in time with it?
The bar to clear
  • A buzz when nothing happened spoils the effect far more than a missing buzz.
  • Precision over recall — this one rule decides everything that follows.

Where this sits

Strand 01

Haptics for spectators

  • All triggered by events; they differ in what they sense — sound, vision, or the body. Iekura 2015 · Handa 2019 · Peng 2022
  • Nearly all need custom hardware. Mizushina 2015 · Takahashi 2021 · de Graaf 2022
  • Earlier Feel the Sports work drove a wrist device from motion sensors. Poutoa 2025 · Nichols et al. 2026
Strand 02

Acoustic detection in tennis

  • Loudness peaks in filtered audio. Caprioli et al. 2024
  • MFCC + CNN, F1 92.4%. Baughman et al. 2019
  • XGBoost, 0.98 for hits — but bounces need several models combined, and real court noise drops it to ~85%. Caprioli et al. 2025
Strand 03

What is missing

  • Those systems study how players perform, not how people watch, and record on court.
  • None deals with TV audio — crowd and commentary over every rally.
  • The closest work using an ordinary phone is outside sport. Gómez-Monroy et al. 2026
The gap Nobody has connected tennis sound detection to vibration on a device the viewer already owns — and 70% of people now carry one.

The sound

Your ear separates them instantly.
The detector cannot.

  • The detector looks for sudden rises in energy.
  • It is very good at that: it finds 97% of every hit a human later confirmed.
  • But a shoe squeak, a bounce, a seat banging and a forehand are all sudden rises.
  • Finding a sound is not the same as knowing what it is.
97% Detector recall of confirmed hits are found
46% Are actually hits 551 of 1,194 checked events — the rest are unwanted
Consequence
  • Fewer than half of what it finds is a racket hit.
  • Buzzing on all of them is not an option.
  • Telling them apart has to come from the sound of the hit itself.

The model

Does it work?

0.87 F1 · racket hit on a match the model never saw while training
0.0 False buzzes / min median across matches, with the detector filtering first
0.68 Strike recall roughly one hit in three still produces no vibration
The architectural finding
  • Testing every 10 ms of audio: 2.4 false buzzes a minute.
  • Let the detector pick the moments first, and ask the model only about those: zero, at the same recall.
  • And 180× less computing.
On honest evaluation
  • Stopping training by looking at the test match made the score 0.10 too high.
  • Splitting the data three ways fixed it.
  • The lower, corrected numbers are the ones shown here.

Why per-broadcast
  • Videos differ hugely in loudness.
  • One fixed setting would leave one match feeling dead and another buzzing constantly.
The honest cost
  • So strength is relative, not absolute.
  • A soft hit in a quiet match can feel like a hard hit in a loud one.
  • Matching across matches is still unsolved.
The feeling

Keeping a phone in time with a laptop

Why not just say “buzz now”?
  • The phone is never told “buzz now”.
  • A message per hit would pick up every wobble in the Wi-Fi — and the timing is the whole point.
  • Sending it all up front leaves the network responsible only for slow drift.
Measured

Timing holds to single-digit milliseconds from laptop to phone.

Where it stands

What is not solved

Limitations
  • Sound alone cannot tell which player hit the ball. Separating them would need a vision model.
  • Buzz strength is relative to each video, not absolute.
  • The videos are highlights, with far less commentary than a live match.
Next
  • Label more of the rare sounds, to catch more hits.
  • Measure it running live — the recordings were pre-processed and reviewed manually.
  • A bigger study, with more people. This seminar is a chance to recruit them.
The principle that held throughout A false buzz is worse than a missed one.
